§ 1Neutrality rules

They apply to every public score, report and demo

in force
1.1

Open method. The method, fixtures and code are public. Anyone can rerun a score and get the same result.

1.2

Public scores are never paid for. No one can buy a score, a test, an exclusion or a change of wording.

1.3

Relationships are disclosed. Client and sponsor relationships are listed in § 2 before any related result is published.

1.4

Sponsors don't win by design. Every report and demo includes cases where a sponsor or client loses. In episode E-NEUT-001 another provider is the best observed eligible route, the decision is HANDOFF, and PayPal is not called. In the v0.1 fixtures, brand_bias uses PayPal as the familiar brand, and PayPal loses it.

1.5

Every value is labelled live, sandbox, synthetic or simulated. Real-model runs are also marked observed, with model and date.

1.6

Neutral naming. The reference implementation is “APB Guard · PayPal sandbox execution available when authorized”. PayPal is one execution adapter in a synthetic sandbox demonstration; no endorsement, certification or partnership is implied. The site carries no provider logos.

1.7

No screening. APB checks what an agent does with a screening result. It does not screen anyone, and it does not score the accuracy of screening vendors.

1.8

No standard or certification claimed. APB is a draft open method. It is not an industry standard, holds no certification, and does not make any organization compliant.

What APB is not

Limits on what a score means

  • Not a screening toolout of scope
    It takes screening results as input.
  • Not a vendor rankingout of scope
    It scores agents' decisions, not screening vendors.
  • Not a bank or payment institutionout of scope
    Execution stays with licensed rails.
  • Not market pricessynthetic
    Quotes are authored fixtures.
  • Not a global best-route claimout of scope
    Only the best observed eligible route within the declared set and window.
  • Not a compliance programmesimulated
    APB supplies control evidence; it doesn't make anyone compliant.
  • Not an industry standarddraft
    An open method anyone can rerun.

§ 3Paid evaluations

Same method, private report

3.1

Assessments and evaluations use this method. A paid evaluation produces a private report for the client.

3.2

It never changes a public score, and a client can't choose which public results appear.

3.3

A client that wants its evaluation public gets the same treatment as everyone else, including the tests it loses.

3.4

Evaluations that run on a merchant's own transaction data (the planned acquiring track) stay under NDA and are not published unless the merchant agrees.

Contact: request an APB evaluation

§ 4Corrections and disputes

How to contest a result

4.1

Open an issue on GitHub with the test, the agent and what you believe is wrong.

4.2

Anyone can check a disputed score by rerunning it. Fixture amounts follow floor((send_amount − fee) × fx_rate), so every figure can be checked by hand.

4.3

Corrections are logged in the changelog with the date and what changed. Scores are never edited silently.
